## Verified technique references (read before any visual production)
1. NSCA, *Basics of Strength and Conditioning*, printed p.55, "4d. Dumbbell Incline Bench Press", https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/116c55d64e1343d2b264e05aaf158a91/basics_of_strength_and_conditioning_manual.pdf. Keep upper arms about 45° to torso; lower dumbbells under control; press back while keeping wrists aligned with elbows.
2. NASM, *Two-Arm Incline Dumbbell Chest Press*, https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/two-arm-incline-dumbbell-chest-press. Its demonstration uses a 45° incline, elbows ~45° from torso, presses up and slightly forward, controls descent toward chest.
3. Rodríguez-Ridao et al. (2020), DOI: 10.3390/ijerph17197339, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33049982/. In that barbell EMG study a 30° incline elicited the most upper-portion activation among tested 0/15/30/45/60° angles. This study does NOT validate any AI-generated video, injured-leg adaptation, or exact dumbbell path.
4. PureGym, *Decline Dumbbell Bench Press*, https://www.puregym.com/exercises/chest/bench-press/decline-dumbbell-bench-press/. The standard setup SECURES FEET UNDER A LEG BRACE; upper back is against a downward-sloping bench; elbows are moderately tucked; lower and raise dumbbells at chest level. It identifies the decline as an advanced exercise.
5. Bench angle comparison, peer-reviewed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25799093/; tested -15°, 0°, 30° and 45° barbell bench positions. Supports clear distinction between decline, flat and incline.

## Exact production movement specifications (not medical clearance)

### 01 INCLINE dumbbell BENCH press
- Bench back support **30° UP from horizontal**. **HEAD HIGHER THAN HIPS** for every frame. No flat bench. Keep full bench, head, torso, dumbbells visible in a static 3/4 side view with a visible horizontal floor reference.
- Anatomical start: shoulders/back remain on the inclined pad; dumbbells near upper chest at sides; palms generally forward or neutral, elbows about 45° to torso; forearms under wrists.
- Rep: two dumbbells travel UP above upper chest, elbows extend under control, dumbbells may converge slightly but must not touch, then lower smoothly to near chest; repeat EXACTLY two complete repetitions. No overhead-shoulder-press motion.
- Video 10–12 seconds, silent, showing bench setup and two complete repetitions. Camera stays fixed. Never swap arms, face, bench angle, equipment, or number of dumbbells.
- **Exact coach pre-set audio cue, separate from footage**: "Incline dumbbell bench press. Set the bench to about thirty degrees, with your head above your hips. Keep your upper back supported. Dumbbells beside the upper chest; press up, then lower slowly. Two controlled repetitions." Existing owner-approved Marcus voice only, after owner approves the wording.
- **Health adaptation hold**: the normal lift uses a stable base. Do not simulate pushing through the immobilized LEFT lower leg, kicking dumbbells into place with that leg, or claiming that the pose is medically safe. Get positioning clearance from treating clinician/qualified rehab coach before promoting an injury-adapted version for follow-along.

### 02 DECLINE dumbbell BENCH press
- Bench **15–20° DOWN toward the HEAD** from horizontal; **HEAD LOWER THAN HIPS** for every frame. Show the complete downward-sloping back pad and floor reference; never let generative model make this a flat bench.
- Anatomical start: shoulder blades supported against decline back pad; dumbbells near sides of chest, elbows moderately tucked; forearms under wrists.
- Rep: press UP above the chest to near elbow extension; lower slowly to chest level, twice. The line of force is vertical relative to gravity, NOT an upright overhead press. Keep torso and angle unchanged.
- **STANDARD DECLINE REQUIRES STABILITY**: ordinary decline benches typically secure legs/feet in a brace. An immobilized LEFT lower leg that must be entirely unloaded may make a conventional decline pressing setup unsuitable. **Do not invent a floating footrest, substitute booted foot anchor, leg restraint, or claim the exercise safe.** A qualified clinician and trainer must approve any feasible supported positioning. Until then, this is an educational movement reference only, NOT a "Marcus follow-along" exercise for an injured participant.
- **Exact coach pre-set audio cue, separate from footage**: "Decline dumbbell bench press. The head end of the bench slopes downward, below the hips. Secure your position before lifting. Keep the dumbbells near chest level, press up under control, then lower slowly for two repetitions." Omit performance invitation if clearance/stabilization is unavailable. Existing owner-approved Marcus voice only, after owner approves the wording.
