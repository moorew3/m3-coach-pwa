## Validated exercise sources (independent of earlier prompts)
- NASM, [Two-Arm Incline Dumbbell Chest Press](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/two-arm-incline-dumbbell-chest-press): uses a **45° upward bench angle**, hands at chest level, elbows roughly 45° from the body, controlled press up/slightly forward and return to chest. It is an exercise illustration with ordinary two-foot stabilization — **not** an injured-leg modification.
- Rodríguez-Ridao et al., 2020, [PMID 33049982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33049982/): in barbell EMG tests of 0°, 15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, the **30°** angle elicited maximal upper-pectoral activity. This does **not** establish a universal best dumbbell angle or validate AI-generated technique.
- PureGym, [Decline Dumbbell Bench Press](https://www.puregym.com/exercises/chest/bench-press/decline-dumbbell-bench-press/): conventional decline is downward toward the head; user securely positions feet under a **leg brace** and presses both dumbbells up, with elbows around 45° and controlled return toward the **mid-chest**. Note this is an advanced exercise.
- Lauver et al., 2015, [PMID 25799093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25799093/): studied -15°, 0°, +30°, +45° barbell bench conditions and found angle-dependent effects; the work must not be treated as a safety test for an immobilized leg.
